---
title: "Sign in to an account again"
sidebarTitle: "Sign in again"
description: "Replace a parked login that expired, went missing or was refused, without signing out of the account in use."
---

Signing in again runs the tool's own sign-in in your browser, as adding an account does. The account keeps its label and its account window.

## Signs that an account needs it

`pitboard` shows one of these after the account's email, then `pitboard enroll work --sign-in`:

- `login expired`
- `nothing parked`
- `login replaced outside Pitboard`

In the app, Pitboard's menu shows **Needs signing in again** under the account. Its row in the Pitboard window has a **Sign In Again** button. [Read the lines under each account](/guides/usage#read-the-lines-under-each-account) says what each means.

A switch to such an account moves nothing and names the same command:

```text
error: the parked login for `work` has expired. Run `pitboard enroll work --sign-in` to sign in to it again.
```

A line that starts `ready · expires in` needs no browser: `pitboard renew` renews that login.

## Sign in again

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="App">
    <Steps>
      <Step title="Open the dialog">
        Click Pitboard's item in the menu bar, then choose the account marked **Needs signing in again**. Or, in the Pitboard window, Control-click the account and choose **Sign In Again**.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Start the sign-in">
        In the **Sign In to work Again** dialog, click **Sign In**. Your browser opens the tool's sign-in.
      </Step>
      <Step title="Sign in">
        Sign in as that account. The dialog closes once the tool has the login.
      </Step>
    </Steps>

    If the browser does not open or a **Code** field appears, see [Add another account](/guides/accounts#add-another-account).
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Command line">
    ```sh
    pitboard enroll work --sign-in
    ```

    For a Codex account, write `codex/work`.

    ```text
    Opening Claude Code's sign-in. Sign in as me@company.com; the account in use now stays signed in.
    ```

    After the sign-in, Pitboard prints:

    ```text
    Renewed work (me@company.com): its parked login is a fresh one.
    ```
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

If you sign in as another account, Pitboard keeps nothing from that sign-in:

```text
error: `work` already refers to me@company.com. Choose a different label.
```

Start again and sign in as `me@company.com`.

## Sign in again to the account in use

The new login takes the place of the one the tool has, and Pitboard parks nothing.

In the Pitboard window, Control-click the account in use and choose **Sign In Again**. After the sign-in, a notice titled **work has a new login** appears.

On the command line, `pitboard enroll work --sign-in` ends with:

```text
Signed in to work (me@company.com) again. Its new login is the one in use now.
```

Everything that runs Codex keeps the old login until it starts again, and can write it back over the new one when it renews. Restart each as [What happens to open sessions](/guides/switch#what-happens-to-open-sessions) says. Pitboard lists the ones still running.

## If Pitboard cannot identify the login in use

Pitboard replaces the login in use only once it has identified it. When it cannot, it parks the new login, and the tool keeps the one it has. That happens when:

- The login in use cannot be read or found.
- A Claude Code login changed since Pitboard last identified it, and Anthropic no longer accepts it or does not answer.

[How Pitboard knows whose login it is](/concepts/switching#how-pitboard-knows-whose-login-it-is) says when Pitboard asks Anthropic. If `work` is the account Pitboard last recorded in use, it also warns:

```text
Renewed work (me@company.com): its parked login is a fresh one.
warning: Claude Code goes on with the login it has: Pitboard could not tell whose it is (its login has expired and cannot be renewed), so it parked the new login for `work` rather than write over that one. If that login no longer works, run `claude` and sign in to `work` there.
```

In the app, the notice is titled **The new login was parked, not put in use**.

To keep parked logins from expiring, see [Keep parked logins alive](/guides/renewal).

title: "How usage is read"
description: "Pitboard reads usage from Anthropic and OpenAI, rereads it only once it could have moved, and estimates how long accounts last."
---

Pitboard asks Anthropic and OpenAI how much of each limit your accounts have used, all at once.

## Where the numbers come from

For the Claude Code account in use, Pitboard asks Anthropic with Claude Code's login; for any other, with its [parked login](/concepts/switching#parked-logins).

For accounts used with OpenAI's Codex CLI, Pitboard sends OpenAI Codex's own usage request, which counts against no limit. Until you enrol a Codex account, Pitboard asks OpenAI nothing.

Work on other machines counts too, as [What a switch costs you](/concepts/limits#what-a-switch-costs-you) explains.

Between answers, Pitboard shows its newest numbers and when they were measured.

## How often Pitboard asks

Pitboard asks about an account again only once 1% of its shortest limit's window has passed. That is 3 minutes for a five-hour limit, about 1 hour 41 minutes for a weekly one and 1 minute with no reading yet.

The command line and the app share one record of when each account was asked. Running `pitboard` twice in a row asks about each account at most once.

When a service asks for less traffic, Pitboard waits the time it names, or else 1 minute, doubling up to 1 hour. If it is unreachable or returns an error, the wait is 30 seconds, doubling up to 15 minutes.

The app and [`pitboard watch`](/reference/account-commands#watch) read usage when they start and every 5 minutes while they run. The app also reads when your Mac wakes. Clicking Pitboard's item in the menu bar reads again if the last read is at least a minute old.

Every 2 seconds, both also look for a switch made elsewhere or numbers that other runs of Pitboard recorded, without asking any service. The app shows what it finds. With [automatic switching](/guides/automatic-switching) on, the app also decides from it whether to switch, as `pitboard watch` does.

When what the app finds says Claude Code's config has named another account since Anthropic last named the login stored, as a sign-in leaves it, the app reads again and asks Anthropic whose the login is. It does so once for each account the config names.

`pitboard status --fresh` and **Refresh** in Pitboard's menu or the **Accounts** pane ask about every account anyway, and try again a service that could not be reached. They do not ask through a wait a service asked for.

## One reading per account

The command line and the app record into one reading per account and show it. Claude Code's status line does too, for Claude Code accounts.

Pitboard's own answer from a service replaces the limits it gives in the account's reading, and can lower a percentage. So when a limit falls in the middle of its window, after a banked reset on claude.ai or a plan upgrade, Pitboard shows the lower percentage the next time it asks. The app asks every 5 minutes.

Anthropic's answer lists every limit an account has, so a limit it leaves out is gone. Where Pitboard cannot read every limit in an answer, it keeps a limit the answer leaves out until it resets.

OpenAI's answer has room for two windows, either of which can be empty. What an empty one means is not known, so Pitboard keeps a window OpenAI leaves out until it resets.

A session's numbers do not say when they were measured or whose they are. They only move a limit the account's last answer gave. Within that limit's window, the higher percentage is the newer, so a percentage a session recorded after the answer was taken stands. Once the window resets, Pitboard takes a session's numbers for the next window. A session never adds a limit.

## Pace

Beside each limit, Pitboard compares its use with an even pace: the same rate from the start of its window to its reset.

At 40% of its weekly limit, an account is fine two days before the reset, and runs out early two days after the week began.

An even pace by now is the part of the window gone by. Halfway through a week it is 50%. Pitboard says the difference in percentage points of the limit:

- `27% over pace`: 27 points more is used than an even pace would have used. At the rate since the window began, the limit runs out before it resets.
- `13% under pace`: 13 points less is used, and the limit lasts until it resets.
- `on pace`: within 5 points either way.

Pitboard says no pace in the first 3% of a window, for a limit used up, or where it does not know when the window resets or how long it runs.

For the account in use, a line names the limit that runs out first at its pace, and when: `weekly limit runs out in 11h 05m at this pace`. An account not in use is not being used, so nothing of it runs out, and in `--json` its `lasts` is its first reset.

Pace is the reading's own: what was used when it was taken, against an even pace at that moment. Between readings, only the time until a limit over pace runs out counts down. An old reading says how old, as in `measured 14:02, 2h 00m ago`.

## What the status line adds

When Claude Code's [status line](/guides/status-line) runs `pitboard statusline`, each session passes it the account's usage. Pitboard records it, so `pitboard`, the app and idle sessions show what busy sessions report.

A session's numbers do not name their account. Pitboard records them only if they moved since the session's last run and Claude Code's config named the same account both times. It records nothing in the 33 seconds an [open session](/concepts/switching#open-sessions) can take to follow a switch.

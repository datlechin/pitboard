The app on Linux is made for GNOME and KDE Plasma, and needs GTK 4.18 and libadwaita 1.7 or later: Debian 13, Ubuntu 26.04 and Fedora 43 or later. It does what [the menu bar app](/reference/mac-app) does, in GNOME's words, and has no Claude Desktop accounts. This page gives where it differs.

Its windows are the Pitboard window and its dialogs: **Preferences**, the [welcome](#welcome), **Open Link**, **Keyboard Shortcuts** and **About Pitboard**. The first time Pitboard runs, the welcome opens over its window. Opened while it runs, Pitboard brings its window to the front.

Closing the window depends on whether the desktop shows Pitboard's [tray icon](#tray-icon):

| The desktop | Closing the window |
| --- | --- |
| Shows the tray icon | Hides the window. Pitboard keeps running until **Quit** in the icon's menu, or Ctrl+Q. If the tray icon goes away, Pitboard quits. |
| Shows no tray icon, as GNOME does without an extension | Quits Pitboard. Turned on, [Watch in the Background](#watch-in-the-background) keeps warming up, and switching where **Limits** says to. |

## Window

The window's header bar holds **Add Account…**, **Refresh**, the main menu, and when Pitboard last read the accounts, such as **Updated 7:08 AM**. It opens on **Accounts**, at the size it had when you last closed it.

**Accounts** lists what the menu bar app's [Accounts window](/reference/mac-app#accounts-window) lists, without Claude Desktop. Each account is a group headed by its name, with its tool and plan, **In Use** or its button, and a menu of its other commands. Its rows are each limit with its bar, then its email address, sign-in and warm-up. [Notices](/reference/mac-app#notices) and [setup tips](/reference/mac-app#setup-tips) come before the accounts.

The main menu holds **Open Link…**, **Diagnostics** and **Activity**, then **Preferences**, **Keyboard Shortcuts**, **Help** and **About Pitboard**. **Diagnostics** and **Activity** open over **Accounts**, with a back button. They show what the menu bar app's [Diagnostics window](/reference/mac-app#diagnostics-window) and [Activity window](/reference/mac-app#activity-window) show, and each reads again every time it opens.

**Open Link…** takes a link pasted with **Paste Link**, or dropped on the window or on **Open Link**, and opens it in your browser. The app on Linux has no [account windows](/reference/account-windows).

## Welcome

The welcome lists what the menu bar app's [welcome window](/reference/mac-app#welcome-window) lists, without Claude Desktop. It says whether Pitboard keeps running once its window is closed. A dialog that **Sign In…** or **Add…** opens comes up over it. **Start Pitboard at Login** starts off. **Continue**, Escape and its close button each close it and show **Accounts**. It does not open again.

## Tray icon

Pitboard shows an icon where the desktop keeps a tray of status icons, as KDE Plasma does. GNOME keeps one only with an extension, such as AppIndicator. The icon comes and goes as the tray does, while Pitboard runs.

The icon shows how much of a limit is used, as [the icon on Windows](/reference/windows-app#icon-in-the-notification-area) does, with a mark of its own while something needs attention. A click opens the Pitboard window, and a right-click opens its menu:

1. **Open Pitboard**.
2. The notices the menu bar app's menu lists.
3. The accounts, under each tool's name, a dot on the account in use, each with the percentage the [menu bar item](/reference/mac-app#menu-bar-item) would show for it.
4. **Add Account…** and **Refresh**.
5. **Preferences** and **Help**.
6. **Quit**.

## Preferences

**Preferences** has the pages **General**, **Limits** and **Advanced**. They hold what [Settings](/reference/settings) holds on macOS, with these differences.

| Page | Control | What differs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **General** | **Start Pitboard at Login** | Turned on, it starts Pitboard with no window each time you log in, as [Start at login](#start-at-login) says. |
| **Limits** | **From** and **To** | Each steps a quarter hour at a time, with its buttons or the arrow keys. You cannot type a time. |
| **Advanced** | **Watch in the Background** | Listed only while the desktop shows no tray icon, as [Watch in the background](#watch-in-the-background) says. |
| **Advanced** | **Uninstall Pitboard…** | Once Pitboard's data is deleted, it removes the app's own files from your home and shows the command that removes the app, as [Uninstall](#uninstall) says. |

**General** has no **Notifications**, **Menu Bar Shows** or **Updates**. Your package manager updates the app.

### Start at login

Pitboard starts at login through an autostart entry, `~/.config/autostart/com.usepitboard.Pitboard.desktop`. The entry starts Pitboard only while the app is installed. If Pitboard cannot write or remove it, the reason shows under the setting.

Started at login, Pitboard opens no window and keeps running only while the desktop shows its tray icon. Where none shows within 10 seconds, it quits, and only [Watch in the Background](#watch-in-the-background) goes on.

### Watch in the background

While the desktop shows no tray icon, nothing of Pitboard runs once its window is closed. **Watch in the Background** has your system's service manager run [the watch service](/reference/account-commands#the-watch-service) from each login. It warms up the accounts **Warm-Up** turns on, and at a limit does what **When a limit reaches** in **Limits** says:

| When a limit reaches | The watch service |
| --- | --- |
| **Switch Automatically** | Switches Claude Code at the share **Limits** has. |
| **Notify Me** | Never switches. Nothing notifies you once the window is closed. |
| **Do Nothing** | Never switches. |

Pitboard installs the service again when you change the choice or the share, and the setting says what the service does.

### Uninstall

**Uninstall Pitboard…** asks first, then deletes Pitboard's saved sign-ins and settings, as on macOS. The app then turns off **Start Pitboard at Login** and removes its own folders, `~/.local/share/com.usepitboard.Pitboard` and `~/.cache/com.usepitboard.Pitboard`.

A dialog then names the command that removes the app and the command line, such as `sudo apt remove pitboard-app pitboard`, with **Copy** beside it. **Quit** closes Pitboard.

## Notifications

The app posts the notifications the [menu bar app](/reference/mac-app#notifications) lists, with the same titles and bodies. The button of **5-Hour Limit Almost Reached** names the account, such as **Switch to personal**. Selecting a notification opens what it opens on macOS.

## Search

While Pitboard runs, GNOME's search and KRunner find **Switch to work** for each account, and **Add Account…**. A word of an account's name finds it. Choosing a switch switches with no window, as [a Jump List switch](/reference/windows-app#jump-list) does on Windows. Neither starts Pitboard.

The app's launcher offers **Add Account…** and **Refresh**, which start Pitboard if it is not running.

## Plasma widget

On KDE Plasma 6.4 or later, the **Pitboard** widget shows the accounts as the [large **Accounts** widget](/reference/mac-app#widgets-and-controls) does on macOS. Debian 13 has Plasma 6.3, and no widget.

In a panel, the widget is the Pitboard mark, which shows the accounts when clicked. **Switch** beside an account switches to it with no window, as a switch from [search](#search) does. A switch Pitboard refuses is said in a notification.

The widget shows what Pitboard shows only while Pitboard runs, and never starts it. While Pitboard is not running, the widget shows only the Pitboard mark, and the system tray keeps it among its hidden items.

## Keyboard shortcuts

**Keyboard Shortcuts** in the main menu lists them.

| Keys | Where | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ctrl+N | The Pitboard window | **Add Account…** |
| Ctrl+R | The Pitboard window | **Refresh** |
| Ctrl+Comma | The Pitboard window | Opens **Preferences** |
| Ctrl+? | The Pitboard window | Opens **Keyboard Shortcuts** |
| F10 | The Pitboard window | Opens the main menu |
| Alt+Left, or Escape | **Diagnostics** and **Activity** | Goes back to **Accounts** |
| Ctrl+W | The Pitboard window | Closes it |
| Ctrl+Q | The Pitboard window | **Quit** |
| Enter | A dialog | Its default button, **Cancel** in **Uninstall Pitboard?** |
| Escape | A dialog | **Cancel**, or closes it |
